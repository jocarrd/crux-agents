crux workspace — isolated workspaces to work in parallel.

  crux workspace new <name> [--repo <dir>] [--base <branch>] [--branch <branch>] [--task <task>] [--goal "<text>"] [--force]
  crux workspace list
  crux workspace resume <task> [--project <p>]   resumes it here from the remote
  crux workspace adopt <path> [--task <task>]    registers a hand-made worktree
  crux workspace heal [--quiet]                  removes entries with no folder or branch
  crux workspace finish <name> [--title "..."]
  crux workspace merge <name> [--yes] [--admin]
  crux workspace rm <name> [--include-ignored]
  crux workspace gc [--yes] [--include-disk]
  crux workspace prune [--yes]

A git worktree isolates files, but not the runtime: two agents on the same
repo still fight over the port, the database and the .env. This shares out
those resources too, with a registry so two workspaces do not take the same
slot.

The worktree is created as a sibling of the original repo, not in a separate
directory: that is what makes the relative context pointers
(../<project>-docs/) keep resolving inside the isolated workspace.

Closing a workspace (rm, and merge, which closes it) deletes its branch, here
and on the remote, when its proposal was merged —squash included— and the
branch holds nothing beyond what was merged. A commit made after the merge
keeps it. The task stops naming the closed workspace, and a deleted branch is
kept as retired so that resuming the task opens a new one.

A workspace's commits leave the machine as they are made: the post-commit
hook publishes the branch in the background to refs/crux/branches/<machine>/
on the remote, which no list of branches shows and no workflow reacts to.
resume brings the most advanced of what the machines published; if the
copies diverged it says so and chooses none. CRUX_MACHINE names the machine
when two share a hostname.

What is not committed travels too: at the end of each agent turn the
workspace's working tree is saved as a commit on its HEAD, to
refs/crux/wip/<machine>/ on the remote. resume applies it when it was saved on
the same commit, and only points at it otherwise. Ignored files, files that
look like a secret (.env, keys), files over 5 MB and more than a thousand new
files are not carried, nor is anything halfway through a merge or in a public
repo. sync --leave saves it too and counts it as travelled.
